## 1.1 What the evidence shows

Continuation-vehicle activity in the Nordic mid-market rose from 4 completed transactions in
2022 to 11 in 2024, measured across the four Nordic markets by the Halden Secondaries Register
[Q1-C01]. Aggregate transaction value over the same period rose from EUR 0.9bn to EUR 3.4bn
[Q1-C02], so average deal size roughly grew alongside deal count rather than in place of it.

Of the 11 transactions completed in 2024, 8 were single-asset continuation vehicles and 3 were
multi-asset [Q1-C03]. The register records sponsor-side advisers on 9 of the 11; in 6 of those
9 the adviser was one of three firms [Q1-C04].

Pricing disclosure is thin. Four of the 11 transactions disclosed a price relative to the most
recent reported net asset value; the disclosed range was 92–101% of NAV [Q1-C05]. The remaining
seven disclosed nothing, and the register marks them `not disclosed` rather than estimating
them.

On the limited-partner side, the Vestbanken LP Sentiment Panel reported that 61% of surveyed
Nordic LPs had participated in at least one GP-led secondary in the preceding 24 months, against
44% in the prior wave [Q2-C01]. The same panel reported that the most frequently cited reason
for declining to roll was insufficient time to underwrite the continuing asset, named by 38% of
non-participating respondents [Q2-C02].

Two structural features recur in the documented transactions. First, status-quo roll options
were offered in 10 of the 11 2024 vehicles [Q2-C03]. Second, where a fairness opinion was
obtained, it was commissioned by the sponsor rather than by the LP advisory committee in every
disclosed instance [Q2-C04].

A supplementary pass found that the Nordic Institutional Investors Forum published guidance on
LP-advisory-committee process for continuation vehicles [GF1-C01].

## 1.2 What this suggests

*The reading below is interpretation. It is separated from § 1.1 deliberately, and no sentence
in this section should be read as a sourced finding.*

The 2022–2024 movement in count and value together [Q1-C01, Q1-C02] is consistent with a route
that has moved from occasional to routine within a narrow band of the market. It is not
evidence that the route is now the dominant liquidity channel — the register captures
continuation vehicles only, so it cannot speak to the relative weight of trade sales or sponsor-
to-sponsor exits.

The single-asset skew [Q1-C03] suggests concentration risk sits with the continuing LP rather
than being diversified away by the structure. That inference is about structure, not about any
particular manager's exercise of it.

The gap between the participation rate [Q2-C01] and the stated reason for declining [Q2-C02] is
the most decision-relevant tension in the chapter. Rising participation alongside a persistent
process complaint is more consistent with LPs accepting a process they consider imperfect than
with the process having improved.

The concentration of sponsor-side advisers [Q1-C04] means the Nordic mid-market operates with a
small number of intermediaries setting process norms, which is why every manager in this segment
runs a materially similar continuation-vehicle process.

Sponsor-commissioned fairness opinions [Q2-C04] are a governance feature worth naming. Whether
they are adequate is a judgement the evidence here does not settle.

## 1.3 Limits of this chapter

Pricing is the weakest area. Seven of eleven transactions disclose no price [Q1-C05], so any
statement about where Nordic continuation vehicles clear relative to NAV rests on four
observations and should be treated as indicative only.

The LP panel [Q2-C01, Q2-C02] is self-selected and its response base is not published. It
supports directional statements about LP posture; it does not support point estimates.

Confirmed evidence scarcity: the pipeline searched for transaction-level fee disclosure across
the register, sponsor filings and adviser publications, and found none. Per the scarcity
register, this chapter applies a SCOPE CAVEAT rather than inferring a fee level. That the
pipeline did not find fee disclosure is a statement about the search, not a finding that fees
were undisclosed.
