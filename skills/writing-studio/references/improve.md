# Improve mode

Use Improve to repair an authorised problem in an existing substantive asset or section. Improve is bounded production work: diagnose the specified problem, preserve locked meaning, and produce a new candidate. It is not cold review or authority to reopen the thesis.

## Validate the revision contract

Locate and read:

- the exact candidate identity and path;
- the stated problem or authorised review findings;
- what must remain unchanged;
- what may change;
- what the revision must accomplish;
- governing evidence, communication policy, and destination constraints;
- the authorised draft and production-record paths; and
- the acceptance owner.

Apply the four-part pre-drafting gate in `SKILL.md` to the revision area. Here, the distinct contribution is the revised section's intended job within the asset; the revision need not invent a new thesis.

If the requested fix would change the thesis, strengthen a claim, alter evidence permission, expand the write boundary, or decide an unowned question, return `SOURCE_OR_DECISION_REQUIRED` rather than treating it as prose work.

## Diagnose before rewriting

Describe why the specified passage is failing against the authorised problem. Distinguish causes such as:

- the block has no clear job or repeats a prior job;
- the reader lacks a prerequisite concept;
- evidence is present but its meaning is not developed;
- the sequence breaks the opening promise or reader journey;
- wording overstates the permitted evidence;
- an observation, interpretation, implication, or qualification has been blurred;
- the form does not fit the block's function; or
- sound content is buried beneath generic or redundant prose.

Stay inside the stated problem. Do not broaden diagnosis into a fresh review of the full asset, issue a verdict, or reopen settled decisions merely because other weaknesses are visible. Record adjacent issues only when destination policy requires them, then leave them with their owner.

## Map preserve, change, and accomplish

Before drafting, write a compact revision map:

- **Preserve:** locked meaning, permitted claims, sound material, required terminology, qualifications, and operator edits.
- **Change:** the exact structure, sequence, wording, evidence treatment, or form authorised for revision.
- **Accomplish:** the observable reader or argument outcome the revised passage must achieve.

Use this map to distinguish a successful revision from a cleaner passage that solves the wrong problem.

## Prepare bounded material

Derive only the material needed from the current candidate, authorised findings, governing sources, and preserved constraints. Treat it as a bounded working pile linked to the source candidate. Do not use fragment exploration to introduce new claims or reopen settled ones.

Use **Shape** by default: establish the reader's current grounding, define the next block's job, and revise in coherent agreed units. Use **Beats** only when the authorised problem concerns narrative order or movement.

The same grounding rule applies as in Develop: a revised unit may rely only on audience prerequisites or concepts already introduced in the retained or revised journey.

## Revise in agreed units

Before every write, re-read the exact current candidate from disk and preserve intervening operator edits. Pause if an edit changes a material claim, the opening promise, or the authorised revision boundary.

For section-sized Shape work, draft the coherent authorised section without requesting approval after every paragraph. Tighten to one block when a load-bearing editorial choice, sensitive claim, evidence gap, or operator checkpoint remains unresolved. For Beats, offer reachable next moves and write only the operator-selected beat.

For each material block, resolve internally:

- what it claims;
- which permitted support it uses;
- what that support means;
- what qualifies the interpretation; and
- what the reader should understand next.

Treat these as reasoning prompts rather than required output fields. While revising, apply thesis progression, analytical value, judgment discipline, calibrated certainty, reader sophistication, and restrained prose.

## Disposition authorised findings

Record one disposition for every authorised finding:

- **applied** — identify the changed candidate unit;
- **not applied** — state the governing reason;
- **returned for authority** — identify the missing decision, evidence, or permission; or
- **superseded** — cite the later authorised direction.

Do not silently ignore a finding or reinterpret it into a different problem. A finding does not itself grant permission to alter a locked claim.

## Bind the new candidate

Create a new candidate identity for a material revision. Record:

- the source candidate;
- the authorised findings or problem statement applied;
- the preserved and changed areas;
- the new candidate path and identifier; and
- any unresolved issue and owner.

Run production QA on the new candidate. Independent review and destination acceptance must bind to the new identity; prior verdicts do not transfer automatically.

## Complete Improve

Improve is complete when the bounded problem has been repaired, locked meaning and operator edits are preserved, every authorised finding has a disposition, and the new candidate is ready for production QA.

If completing the revision would require new evidence, a changed thesis, broader claim permission, or an unsafe write, return `SOURCE_OR_DECISION_REQUIRED` with the stop contract from `SKILL.md`.
